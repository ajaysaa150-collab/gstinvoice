EDUCATIONAL GUIDE

Understanding Indian GST Invoicing

Essential principles, statutory requirements, and calculation standards for Indian businesses issuing tax invoices under the Goods and Services Tax framework.

What is a GST Invoice?

A GST invoice is an official commercial document issued by a registered supplier to a recipient of goods or services. It acts as legal evidence of the supply, records the applicable tax liability, and allows registered buyers to claim an Input Tax Credit (ITC) under Indian tax laws.

Who Needs a GST Invoice?

Any business, freelancer, consultant, or agency registered under the Goods and Services Tax regime must issue a GST invoice when supplying taxable goods or services. Taxable individuals cannot collect GST from buyers without delivering a formal tax invoice containing their valid 15-digit GSTIN.

What Should a GST Invoice Include?

Under GST rules, a valid invoice should contain mandatory data points:

  • Consecutive serial number and invoice date
  • Supplier name, registered address, and GSTIN
  • Recipient name, address, and GSTIN (for registered buyers)
  • Itemized description, quantity, unit rate, and total value
  • Taxable value after discounts, along with applicable tax rates
  • Clear separation of CGST, SGST, or IGST amounts

How GST Is Calculated

GST calculation depends on whether the transaction is Intra-State (within the same state) or Inter-State (between two different states):

  • Intra-State: Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST) are split equally (e.g., 18% GST = 9% CGST + 9% SGST).
  • Inter-State: Integrated GST (IGST) is charged entirely at the full slab rate (e.g., 18% IGST).

Regulatory Notice

GST regulations and notification schedules are subject to periodic changes by the GST Council and CBIC. Always verify that your HSN/SAC codes, reverse charge obligations, and e-invoicing limits comply with the latest rules for your business turnover tier.
